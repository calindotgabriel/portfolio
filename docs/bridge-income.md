# Bridge Income: Decision Rules

Written on 2026-08-07 with 1–2 months of runway, so that this decision is made **in advance and
calmly**, not later and under pressure. Panic picks the worst instrument.

## The Principle

**Training is the asset. The bridge exists to protect it, not to replace it.**

The search is failing at interview conversion (`market-fit-plan.md`), and the remedy is ~3 hours a day
of deliberate practice for 8 weeks (`training-plan.md`). Any bridge income that consumes those hours
or the energy behind them is not a bridge — it is a slower version of the same problem, with a second
unexplained gap on the CV at the end of it.

So every option below is judged on two axes: **cash per hour**, and **damage to training capacity**.

---

## eMAG Marketplace / online commerce — **no**

Not "not now" — wrong instrument for this problem.

- Requires PFA or SRL setup, an upfront inventory purchase, and working capital before the first leu
  comes back.
- Typically 3–6 months to profitability against thin margins and established sellers with better
  buying power.
- **The timeline is longer than the runway, and the capital requirement points the wrong way:** it
  spends savings during exactly the window those savings exist to cover.
- It is also a full second job cognitively — sourcing, listings, pricing, customer service, returns —
  which is precisely the training capacity that cannot be spared.

An e-commerce business may be a good idea in some other year. It is not a way to cover a 1–2 month
gap, and starting one now would convert a solvable interview problem into an unsolvable cash problem.

## Gardening / manual services — **emergency valve only**

Genuinely viable as a valve, genuinely bad as a plan.

**For:**
- Near-zero startup cost. Cash within days, not months.
- August–October is still in season in Cluj.
- No capital at risk.

**Against:**
- Caps around 50–80 RON/hour.
- Physically tiring in a way that directly degrades 3 hours of daily cognitive practice. This is the
  real cost, and it is larger than it looks.
- Seasonal — it ends in November, which is roughly when the runway question would recur.
- Adds a second gap to explain, on a CV that already carries two.

**Use only if the trigger below fires**, and even then capped so training survives.

## Tech work at a lower rate — **the actual answer**

Already the accepted commercial posture (`sales/core-proposal.md` v1.1: €35/hour long-term floor,
€25/hour bridge floor).

- Upwork, Toptal, freelancermap, short fixed-scope engagements, part-time or hourly Node/TS work.
- **€25–35/hour beats gardening by 3–5× per hour** and keeps the CV continuous instead of introducing
  a second gap.
- It compounds with the main plan rather than competing with it: billable work is the strongest
  available answer to the seniority question, and paid delivery under someone else's standards is
  itself interview material.
- Part-time is fine and preferable. 15–20 billable hours a week at €30/hour covers a lot while leaving
  the training block intact.

This is where bridge effort goes first, second, and third.

## The Trigger Rule

Decided now, executed without re-litigating:

| Runway remaining | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **> 6 weeks** | Full plan. Training + applications only. No bridge activity. |
| **4–6 weeks** | Open the low-rate tech bridge in parallel: Upwork/freelancermap profiles live, actively bidding on short engagements. Training hours unchanged. |
| **< 4 weeks** | Activate the manual-services valve at a **capped** number of hours per week — capped specifically so the daily training block survives intact. Keep bidding on tech work. |
| **< 2 weeks** | Cap comes off manual services. Training drops to the live-coding block only (60–90 min), which is the part that decays fastest without practice. |

Two things never get cut, at any runway level:

1. **The interview post-mortems.** They cost 20 minutes and they are the only data on the failing stage.
2. **The mock interviews.** They are the highest-value hour in the week and the first thing that a
   busy schedule quietly deletes.
